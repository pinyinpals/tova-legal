Translate Japanese signs and station boards

Station boards, exit signs, shop notices and warning placards are where a trip to Japan actually gets hard. Point Tova at one and it reads back in English with romaji above every character.

How it works

Read it in three steps

  1. Set the scan language to Japanese and point the camera at the sign.
  2. Tap the shutter — Tova returns the English meaning plus romaji over each kana and kanji.
  3. Save the ones you will need again, like your station name or hotel address, to Favourites.
Common questions

About this guide

How do I translate a Japanese sign with my phone?

Open Tova, set the camera scan language to Japanese, point at the sign and tap the shutter. You get the English meaning and romaji above every character, so you can also say the place name aloud when you ask for directions.

Will it read vertical Japanese text?

Yes. Vertical layouts are common on signage and shopfronts, and Tova's recogniser handles them alongside horizontal text.

Does it work on the small print on station boards?

Zoom in first. After you take the shot you can pinch to magnify the photo and translate just the region you are looking at, which is far more reliable than trying to scan a whole crowded board at once.

Do I need a data connection in Japan?

Not for recognising the text — that happens on your phone. If you have no data, translation falls back to your device's on-device engine, so you still get a reading.
