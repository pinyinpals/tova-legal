Tova vs Pleco: a dictionary and a translator, compared honestly

By ZET Studios · Tested September 2026 · Updated 8 November 2026

Pleco is the dictionary most serious learners of Chinese swear by, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. The question is a narrower one: if you are travelling in China, which of the two answers the thing in front of you?

The short answer

Learning Chinese? Get Pleco. Its dictionaries, stroke order, handwriting lookup and flashcards are a decade ahead of anything else, and they all run offline. Some of them, including flashcards and full stroke order, are paid add-ons.

Travelling and need sentences, menus and conversations? That is what Tova does. Point the camera at a sign and you get the whole line translated, with pinyin above every character. Speak English and it says it back in Chinese.

Plenty of people carry both. They are not really competing for the same job, and the rest of this page is about where the line between them falls.

What people say when Pleco is the wrong tool

Pleco is rated 4.7 on the App Store and its low-star reviews are rare. Most of the few that exist come from the same place: someone downloaded a dictionary expecting a translator. We read every one- and two-star App Store review in four English-speaking storefronts, and three themes repeat.

  • “Not a practical travel translation app.” A UK reviewer in October 2026 wrote that for “menus, signs and asking for directions” they would not recommend it. Another could not get a sentence like “which way is the bus stop” translated. Pleco looks up words; it does not translate sentences, and it does not say so loudly enough for a first-time visitor.
  • The camera costs extra. Pleco's own listing marks OCR with a ($). The Optical Character Recognizer is a $9.99 add-on, or part of the $29.99 Basic and $59.99 Professional bundles. Several reviewers were unhappy that handwriting recognition moved from free to paid.
  • It takes some learning. Pleco is built for students and has the depth to match. Reviewers who wanted to point and go called it “not user-friendly” and hard to set up.

Where Pleco wins, and it is not close

Looking up a single character. Draw it, search by radical, type pinyin without tones, or tap it in a photo. Pleco gives you every reading, several dictionaries' worth of definitions, example sentences and the stroke order. Tova tells you what the sign says; Pleco tells you everything about the third character on it.

Studying. Flashcards, graded readers and a document reader turn what you look up into what you remember. Tova has nothing comparable, and the Saved tab keeps five phrases on the free tier.

No ads and no subscription. Pleco's free app has no advertising, and its add-ons are one-time purchases that its developers have kept updating for years. Tova's free tier shows ads, and its paid tier is a subscription or a time-limited pass.

Offline by design. Everything in Pleco lives on the phone. Tova reaches its servers when it can and only falls back to on-device translation when it cannot.

Where Tova is the better fit

A whole menu, not one word. One capture reads every line on the board and gives you the dish names in English with the pinyin to order them. With Pleco you would move the crosshair across the menu one word at a time. The Chinese menu guide shows what that looks like.

A conversation. Tova's Speak tab listens in English and says it back in Chinese, then does the reverse for the person you are talking to. Pleco does not do two-way speech.

The camera is free. Scanning with readings above every character is part of the free app, not an add-on.

The next country. Tova reads Japanese with romaji, Korean with romanization and Hong Kong Cantonese with Jyutping. Pleco is a Chinese dictionary, and does that one language very well.

Using both on one trip

This is what a lot of learners end up doing. Tova handles the menu, the station sign and the taxi driver. When a character on the sign catches your eye, look it up in Pleco to find out why it is pronounced that way and what else it means.

Both work in mainland China. Pleco needs no connection at all. Tova reaches its own servers without a VPN and falls back to the phone when there is no signal. Install both before you go: on Android, Google Play is blocked in mainland China, so neither app can be installed after you land.

Common questions

About this guide

Is Pleco a translator?

No. Pleco is a Chinese-English dictionary. It looks up words and characters, by typing, drawing or pointing the camera at them, but it does not translate whole sentences or hold a conversation.

Is Pleco's camera free?

No. On the US App Store the Optical Character Recognizer is a $9.99 add-on, also included in the $29.99 Basic and $59.99 Professional bundles. The base dictionary app is free.

Does Pleco work in China without a VPN?

Yes. Pleco's dictionaries are stored on your phone, so it works with no connection at all.

Does Tova replace Pleco?

Not for studying. Tova translates signs, menus and speech and shows pinyin above each character, but it has no dictionary entries, stroke order or flashcards. Learners usually keep Pleco for that.

Is Tova free?

Yes, with ads. Camera scanning, per-character readings, translation into 118 languages and two-way voice are free. Pro removes ads and adds features such as Boardroom three-way speech and Common Replies, for $7.99 a month, $49.99 a year, or a one-off $4.99 seven-day Travel Pass (US prices).
