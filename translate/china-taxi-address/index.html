Show a taxi driver your address in Chinese (and read a Chinese address in English)

By ZET Studios · Updated 17 November 2026

A driver in China finds a place fastest from its name and address in Chinese characters. Get that address from the hotel or the place you are going, keep it on your screen, and hold it up. Here is where to find it, the ride apps that work in English, and how to read a Chinese address the other way round.

The short answer

Show the driver the address in Chinese characters, on paper or on your screen. Not in English, and not in pinyin: pinyin spells out the sounds, and many different characters share each sound, so it does not tell the reader which place you mean. The most reliable copy comes from the place itself: the hotel’s Chinese website, your booking confirmation if it shows the Chinese address, or a card from reception. Screenshot it so it still works with no signal.

If you would rather not hail a taxi on the street, ride-hailing apps work in English. DiDi’s listing describes an English interface, sign-in with a foreign mobile number and payment by international bank card (App Store). Amap (Gaode), the big Chinese map app, has an English version, added 14 more languages in July 2025, and includes an English ride-hailing screen that books cars from several providers (Shanghai government). Alipay has added translation into 16 languages for some services, ride-hailing among them (People’s Daily, published by the People’s Bank of China).

Reading a Chinese address goes the other way. It is written from the biggest place to the smallest, city first and house number last, and the English version turns that order upside down. The sections below go through both.

Get the address in Chinese before you need it

Don’t build the address yourself from English. An English address spells street names in pinyin, and pinyin leaves out which characters were meant, so a translator turning it back into Chinese has to guess. Use the Chinese address the place gives you.

  • Your hotel. Copy the name and address in characters from the hotel’s own Chinese page or your booking confirmation, or ask reception to write it down on your first day. Take a hotel card if there is one.
  • Everywhere else you plan to go. Restaurants, sights, stations: look the place up in a map app and copy its Chinese name and address before you set out.
  • Put the name above the address. Big hotels, malls and stations are often known by name. Show the place name in characters first, then the street address.
  • Screenshot it, and keep a paper copy of the hotel. A screenshot works with no signal; paper works with a flat battery.

Showing it to the driver

  1. Have the address on screen before you get in. Hold it up in large type, place name first.
  2. Say it if you can. Read the place name aloud using the pinyin, then point at the screen. A wrong tone matters less when the characters are in front of the driver.
  3. In a ride-hailing app, paste the Chinese name as the destination, then check the pin on the map before you confirm. Two places can share a name in different districts.
  4. Keep the way back ready. Before you go out, make sure the hotel’s address is the first thing in your photos or saved phrases.

How to read a Chinese address in English

A Chinese address starts with the biggest place and ends with the smallest: province or city, then district, road and number, then building and room. Written in English the order is reversed, smallest first. That is the format China Post gives to the Universal Postal Union, along with this example: No. 12 Zhichun Road, Haidian District, Beijing, with the six-digit postcode written before the province or city name (UPU, China addressing guide).

In characters, that address is 北京市海淀区知春路12号. Read it in chunks:

ChinesePinyinPartIn English
北京市Běijīng ShìCity (here also the province level)Beijing
海淀区Hǎidiàn QūDistrictHaidian District
知春路Zhīchūn LùRoadZhichun Road
12号shí’èr hàoNumberNo. 12

Notice what stays the same: the street name is kept in pinyin (Zhichun), and only the word for the kind of road becomes English. China Post’s own pinyin version keeps even that: Zhichun Lu 12 Hao, Haidian Qu. So if a translator turns a street name into English words, go back to the pinyin; that is the spelling a map app or an address in English will use.

Common questions

About this guide

How do I tell a taxi driver in China where to go?

Show the address in Chinese characters, on your screen or on paper. Get it from the hotel or the place you are going rather than translating an English address yourself, and screenshot it so it works without a signal. If you can, say the place name out loud too, using its pinyin.

How do I translate a Chinese address into English?

Read it from the end. A Chinese address runs from the largest place to the smallest, city or province first and house number last, and in English the order is reversed. So 北京市海淀区知春路12号 becomes No. 12 Zhichun Road, Haidian District, Beijing. The street name stays in pinyin; words like 路 (road), 区 (district) and 号 (number) are the ones that change.

Can foreigners use DiDi in China?

DiDi’s App Store listing describes an English interface, sign-in with a foreign mobile number, and payment with international bank cards. Amap and Alipay also offer ride-hailing with English. Set the app up before you fly, and enter your destination in Chinese characters.

How do you say taxi driver in Chinese?

出租车司机 (chūzūchē sījī). To speak to the driver, use 师傅 (shīfu), a polite way to address a skilled worker. 去这里 (qù zhèlǐ) means “take me here”.

Does Tova work in China without a VPN?

Yes. When Tova cannot reach a server it translates on your phone instead, so the camera and the pinyin keep working with no VPN and no signal. Set up offline translation on home Wi-Fi before you travel.
