Cantonese is not Mandarin with an accent

Cantonese is the everyday language of Hong Kong, Macau and much of Guangdong, including Guangzhou. It shares most of its characters with Mandarin, but the sounds are different enough that a Mandarin speaker and a Cantonese speaker cannot follow each other in conversation.

That is the problem with using Hanyu Pinyin in Hong Kong. Pinyin is built for Mandarin. Put pinyin above a Cantonese menu and you get the Mandarin reading of every character, which is not what anyone in the restaurant will say. 飲茶, the word for going out for dim sum, is yǐn chá in Mandarin and jam2 caa4 in Cantonese. Say the first in a Hong Kong teahouse and you will be understood only because the staff guess.

Jyutping is the romanisation the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong designed for Cantonese. It is what Tova uses when you set the language to Cantonese, and it is what the free Jyutping converter on this site produces from any text you paste.

The six tones, briefly

Jyutping marks tone with a number after each syllable rather than an accent over a vowel. Cantonese has six tones, and changing the tone changes the word. The classic set uses the syllable si:

CharactersJyutpingMeaning
詩si1Tone 1, high level — poem
史si2Tone 2, high rising — history
試si3Tone 3, mid level — try
時si4Tone 4, low falling — time
市si5Tone 5, low rising — market, city
事si6Tone 6, low level — matter, thing

Ordering dim sum

  1. Find the order sheet. Most dim sum restaurants give you a printed sheet listing every dish, with a box to write the quantity. It is almost entirely characters.
  2. Capture the whole sheet. Tap the shutter once and Tova reads the full sheet, with Jyutping above each dish name and the English underneath. For the broader menu vocabulary — cooking methods, proteins, warning words — see how to read a Chinese menu.
  3. Mark the boxes, hand it over. You do not have to say anything. When a trolley or server comes past, 呢個 (ni1 go3, this one) and a point is enough.
  4. Ask for the bill with 唔該埋單. m4 goi1 maai4 daan1. A raised hand and a small scribbling gesture works too.

Taking a Hong Kong minibus

Minibuses do not stop at every stop unless someone asks. The route and fare are on a card at the front, usually in characters, so capture it before you get on if you are unsure you have the right one.

When your stop is coming, call out 有落 (jau5 lok6) — getting off — or 前面有落 (cin4 min6 jau5 lok6), getting off ahead. This is the one place where saying it matters more than showing it: the driver is watching the road, not your phone. Practise it with the Jyutping first and tap Listen to hear it.

Common mistakes

  • Using pinyin in Hong Kong. It gives you the Mandarin reading. Many people in Hong Kong speak Mandarin, but it is not the language of the street.
  • Dropping the tone numbers. Without them, si could be any of six words. The numbers are the pronunciation, not decoration.
  • Mixing up 唔該 and 多謝. Both mean thank you. 唔該 is for a service — someone holding a door, bringing your food. 多謝 is for a gift or a favour.
  • Reading Jyutping as English. j sounds like English y, so jau5 is close to "yow". c is like ts or ch, so caa4 is close to "chah". eo and oe are vowel sounds with no English equivalent.
  • Expecting simplified characters. Hong Kong and Macau use traditional characters. Guangzhou uses simplified, but the spoken language there is still Cantonese.

About this guide

What's the best Cantonese translator app with Jyutping?

Tova Translate shows Jyutping above every Cantonese character across camera, voice and text, rather than relabelling Mandarin pinyin. It is free to download on iPhone, iPad and Android and works across Hong Kong, Macau and Guangdong.

Does Tova show Jyutping or just pinyin?

Jyutping for Cantonese, with tone numbers. Mandarin gets Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks separately. You choose which by setting the language to Cantonese or Mandarin.

Why is pinyin wrong for Cantonese?

Pinyin records Mandarin sounds. Cantonese reads the same characters differently and has six tones rather than four, so pinyin gives you the Mandarin pronunciation of a Cantonese sentence, which is not what people in Hong Kong say.

Will it work in Hong Kong and Macau?

Yes. Tova reads traditional characters, which Hong Kong and Macau use, and when there is no signal it falls back to on-device translation with Jyutping still shown.

Can it speak Cantonese aloud?

Yes. Tap Listen to hear a result read aloud, so you can use it with taxi drivers and shopkeepers, not just to read.

What do the numbers in Jyutping mean?

They are the tone. Cantonese has six: 1 high level, 2 high rising, 3 mid level, 4 low falling, 5 low rising and 6 low level. The same syllable with a different number is a different word.
