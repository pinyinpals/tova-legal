China for gamers: esports hotels, arenas and anime shops

Last updated: November 2026

China has more esports hotels than any other country, a pro league with home arenas in five cities, and whole malls given over to anime figures and game goods. Here is how to plan a trip around them as a foreign visitor, including the parts that still need a Chinese ID.

Book an esports hotel

An esports hotel (电竞酒店, diànjìng jiǔdiàn) is a hotel whose rooms come with gaming PCs, high-refresh monitors and proper chairs, often two to five machines in one room so a group can play together. There are more than 20,000 of them in China. Some are small independent places, others are rooms set aside inside ordinary chain hotels.

Most of the ones you can book in English take foreign guests. On 20 November 2026 we opened every hotel on Trip.com’s esports pages for the nine cities below, 348 listings in all, and all but two said guests from all countries and regions are welcome. The exceptions are real, though: some esports hotels on the site only take mainland Chinese ID cards. Each hotel page has a Guests Accepted line, and the city lists don’t show it, so open the hotel and read it before you pay.

Most dedicated esports hotels are adults only: 50 of the 56 we checked require the guest checking in to be 18 or over.

Look for “Guests from all countries/regions are welcome” on the hotel page. If it says the hotel only accepts mainland Chinese ID cards, pick another.

Stay near the big venues

A few arenas keep coming back for the biggest events, and they are worth a visit even on a day with nothing on.

Mercedes-Benz Arena, Shanghai (梅赛德斯-奔驰文化中心) hosted Dota 2’s The International in 2019 and still holds esports finals and concerts. Hotels near Mercedes-Benz Arena.

Beijing National Stadium, the Bird’s Nest (国家体育场, 鸟巢) held the League of Legends World Championship final in 2017 and the KPL annual final in 2025. Hotels around the Olympic park.

Hangzhou Esports Center (杭州电竞中心), in Gongshu district, was built for esports at the 2022 Asian Games (held in 2023) and kept as an esports venue afterwards. Esports hotels in Hangzhou.

Get match tickets

LPL tickets are sold only on two Chinese ticket apps, Piaoxingqiu (票星球) and Maoyan (猫眼), and through their WeChat mini programs. The official site and the league’s WeChat account send you there.

Every ticket is bound to one person’s ID, and the league’s 2026 notice says overseas fans may use a foreign passport. You can buy at most two tickets per match, the name is checked against your ID at the gate, and tickets can’t be changed once bought. Payment is through WeChat Pay or Alipay, so set one of them up with your foreign card before you try. Commentary in the arena is in Chinese.

For KPL matches, the notices we read asked for an ID card and did not mention passports. Ask the seller before you buy, or buy through a ticket agent who has done it before.

Big finals sell out in minutes. Have the ticket app installed, your passport added as a real-name ID and WeChat Pay or Alipay working before sales open.

Before you play online

  • Internet cafés (网吧, wǎngbā) scan an ID for every customer. Bring your passport, but expect that some cafés only have readers for Chinese ID cards and will say no.
  • Chinese game accounts use real-name registration tied to Chinese ID. Whether a foreign passport works depends on the game, so don’t count on making a new account for a Chinese server while you’re there.
  • Teen travellers: under-18s on Chinese servers may only play from 8 to 9pm on Fridays, weekends and public holidays.
  • Your own accounts: many foreign sites and services are filtered in mainland China, and unapproved VPNs are not permitted under Chinese rules. Visitors commonly report that a roaming SIM or a travel eSIM reaches home services, but plan for some games, voice chat and stores not to connect, and expect high ping to servers back home.
  • Payments: link your foreign Visa or Mastercard to Alipay or WeChat Pay at home. Tickets, arcades and most shops expect one of them.

Common questions

About this guide

Can foreigners stay in esports hotels in China?

Most of the ones you can book in English do. When we checked 348 hotels on Trip.com’s esports pages in nine cities in November 2026, all but two welcomed guests from all countries; the two exceptions only take mainland Chinese ID cards. Read the Guests Accepted line on the hotel page before you pay. Most dedicated esports hotels also require the guest to be 18 or over.

Can I buy LPL tickets with a foreign passport?

Yes. The league’s 2026 ticket notice says overseas fans may use a foreign passport as their real-name ID. Tickets are sold on the Piaoxingqiu and Maoyan apps, two per person per match, and are paid for with WeChat Pay or Alipay.

Where can I buy anime figures in Shanghai?

Bailian ZX on Nanjing Road East (百联ZX创趣场) is a whole mall of anime and game shops, with a second branch at Wujiaochang. It is the easiest single stop for figures and official goods.

When is ChinaJoy?

ChinaJoy is held in Shanghai at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre, usually in late July or early August. Bilibili World, the other big Shanghai show, is in July.

What esports events are coming up in China?

Announced so far: the CrossFire Stars and Overwatch world finals in late 2026 (Wuhan and Guangzhou), the Shanghai Esports Masters in December 2026, Valorant’s China league stages in Hangzhou, Wuhan and Shanghai in 2027, IEM Beijing, BLAST in Hong Kong and the Counter-Strike Major in Shanghai in late 2027. The calendar on this page lists dates and venues and is checked regularly.
