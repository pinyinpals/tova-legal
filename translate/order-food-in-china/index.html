How to order food in China as a foreigner

By ZET Studios · Updated 17 November 2026

In many restaurants in China the menu is a QR code on the table: you scan it with WeChat or Alipay, order on your own phone and pay in the same screen. Here is how that works for a visitor, what to do when it asks for a Chinese phone number, and how to read a menu that is only in Chinese.

The short answer

Ordering in China works in one of three ways, sometimes all in the same restaurant: a QR code on the table that opens the menu on your phone, a paper or picture menu, or a counter where you point and pay. QR ordering usually runs inside WeChat or Alipay as a mini-program, a small app that opens inside the big one, so there is nothing extra to install (Sixth Tone).

You can do all of it as a visitor. Alipay and WeChat Pay accept foreign bank cards, and foreigners can open mobile payment in China with an overseas phone number (People’s Daily, published by the People’s Bank of China). When a QR menu gets stuck, ask for a paper menu or order at the counter, and keep some cash: the central bank’s stated line is “no refusal to accept cash” (People’s Bank of China briefing, March 2024).

How QR-code ordering works

  1. Find the code. It is usually on a stand or a sticker on the table, sometimes with a table number beside it.
  2. Scan it from inside Alipay or WeChat, with the app’s own Scan button. The restaurant’s menu opens inside the app, usually with a photo of each dish.
  3. Add dishes to the basket, then check it before you confirm.
  4. Pay in the same screen, with the card linked to the app. The order is placed without you having to flag anyone down.

When it asks for a phone number or to follow an account

Not every restaurant stops at the menu. When reporters looked at 72 restaurants with scan-to-order in 2021, 35 made customers follow the restaurant’s WeChat account first, and 20 asked for access to the phone number linked to the customer’s WeChat account (Sixth Tone). In 2023 a Shanghai chain was reported to have no paper menus at all, with a membership sign-up as the only way to order (China Daily).

Regulators have pushed back: the same China Daily report says Shanghai’s market regulator fined a catering company 50,000 yuan in 2021 for collecting customers’ personal information through QR-code ordering. You will still meet these screens. If one stops you, there are other ways in:

  • Ask for a paper menu. Some places keep one for customers who ask.
  • Order at the counter, or wave someone over and point.
  • Ask the staff to order for you. China Daily found coffee-shop staff placing orders on their own phones for customers who would not use the app.

Paying as a foreigner

Alipay and WeChat Pay both let foreign users link international cards, including Visa and Mastercard (gov.cn, April 2024). Set them up at home, where you can still receive the verification text on your usual number, and check with your bank that the card works abroad. The China travel checklist covers the setup step by step.

In 2024 the People’s Bank of China raised the single-transaction limit for overseas visitors using mobile payment from US$1,000 to US$5,000, which covers any normal restaurant bill (PBOC briefing).

Cards and cash still work in many places. A card is accepted where its network’s logo is shown at the till, UnionPay cards are accepted at every merchant terminal (gov.cn), and the central bank has said it will act against merchants who refuse cash. Carry a little in yuan for the times the app does not cooperate.

Alipay has added translation into 16 languages for some of its services, such as ride-hailing and hotel booking (People’s Daily). A restaurant’s own menu page inside it may still be only in Chinese, which is where the next section comes in.

Reading a QR menu you cannot read

A QR menu is Chinese text on your own screen, so you cannot easily point a camera at it. Screenshot it instead.

  1. Screenshot the menu inside WeChat or Alipay. If it is long, scroll and take a few.
  2. Open Tova, go to the Camera tab and pick the screenshot from your photos. Each line comes back in English, with Hanyu Pinyin above every character.
  3. Zoom in on one part. Pinch to zoom on a section and tap Translate this view, or use Crop & Translate to pick out a single dish.
  4. Go back and order. Match the characters on the menu to the ones in your translation; the first two characters of a dish name are usually enough to find it.

A paper menu is quicker still: point the camera at it and tap the shutter, and the whole page comes back at once. In the live camera view, each line Tova reads has a button that shows it in large type, to hold up for the waiter. The pinyin means you can also say the dish out loud, which helps when the waiter comes to check. For the forty-odd characters that do most of the work on any menu, see how to read a Chinese menu.

When the signal drops, or Google is blocked

Google’s services are blocked in mainland China, so Google Translate cannot reach its servers there without a VPN. Tova keeps translating when it cannot reach a server: the translation runs on your phone, and the pinyin is still there. Set this up on home Wi-Fi before you fly; the offline translator guide shows what to download, and translating in China without a VPN explains what does and does not work once you are there.

The menu explainer, and allergies

Tova Pro adds Explain this menu. After a capture that looks like a menu, it goes through the dishes one by one: what each one is, how it is usually made, the allergens it commonly contains and how spicy it tends to be. Pro can also show any translation full screen and flip it to face the person across the table.

Treat the explainer as a first look, not a safety check. It describes how a dish is usually made, kitchens differ, and it can miss an ingredient.

Do not rely on a phone for a severe allergy. Machine translation drops negations and kitchens cross-contaminate. If a reaction would put you in hospital, carry a card written by a native speaker, show it, and ask the staff.

Common questions

About this guide

How do you order food in China if you don’t speak Chinese?

Many restaurants put a QR code on the table: scan it with Alipay or WeChat, pick dishes from the menu that opens, and pay in the same screen. Where there is no code, point at a picture menu or at a dish on another table. To read a menu that is only in Chinese, screenshot it and open the screenshot in a translator such as Tova, which shows the English and the pinyin for each line.

Can I order from a QR code without a Chinese phone number?

Often, yes: the menu opens inside Alipay or WeChat, and foreigners can open mobile payment in China with an overseas phone number. Some restaurants also ask you to share your phone number or follow their WeChat account before you can order. If you get stuck at that step, ask for a paper menu, order at the counter, or ask the staff to order for you.

Can foreigners pay with Alipay or WeChat Pay?

Yes. Both let visitors link foreign bank cards, including Visa and Mastercard. Set them up before you fly, while you can still receive verification texts on your usual number, and carry some cash as a backup: China’s central bank has said it will act against merchants who refuse cash.

Does Tova work in China without a VPN?

Yes. When Tova cannot reach a server it translates on your phone instead, so the camera and the pinyin keep working with no VPN and no signal. Set up offline translation on home Wi-Fi before you travel.

Is the menu explainer free?
